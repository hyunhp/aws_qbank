@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.1 advanced use-case
S: A regional bank has 14 proposed AI projects and budget for three this year. The CEO asks for a way to choose that the board will accept. Which prioritization approach is most defensible?
A: Score each project on expected business value, feasibility with current data and skills, and risk, then pick the top three
B: Fund the three projects that use the most advanced AI techniques, since those create the strongest market perception of innovation
C: Let each department vote, and fund the three projects that receive the most internal votes this quarter
D: Start with the three cheapest projects so the bank can show quick spending discipline to the board members
K: A
W: A value-feasibility-risk matrix ties each project to measurable outcomes and realistic delivery, which gives the board a transparent reason for each choice.
X: B=Technical novelty is not a business outcome and often raises risk. | C=Votes measure enthusiasm, not value or feasibility. | D=Low cost alone ignores whether a project delivers anything.
T: Prioritize AI initiatives by value, feasibility, and risk together, not by novelty, popularity, or price.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 applied use-case
S: A support organization launched an AI assistant that drafts replies for agents. Leadership wants proof of value after 90 days. What must have been captured before launch for that proof to be credible?
A: Baseline metrics such as average handling time and resolution rate from before the assistant was introduced
B: The number of prompts the development team tested while building the assistant during its pilot phase
C: The list of foundation models evaluated during vendor selection, along with their published benchmark scores
D: A survey of executives asking how much value they expect the assistant to create over the next year
K: A
W: Value is shown as change against a starting point. Without pre-launch baselines for the target metrics, improvements after launch cannot be attributed or quantified.
X: B=Prompt counts describe development effort, not business impact. | C=Benchmarks describe models, not results in this organization. | D=Expectations are not measurements.
T: Measure the baseline before launch, or you cannot prove the change afterward.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced tradeoff
S: An AI invoice-processing tool cost $400,000 to deploy and $100,000 a year to run. It saves 12,000 staff hours a year at a loaded cost of $50 per hour. Which statement about the first-year return is accurate?
A: Annual savings are $600,000, so the first year returns about $100,000 net after deployment and running costs
B: Annual savings are $600,000, so the first year returns $500,000 net because deployment is a one-time cost
C: The tool loses money in year one because staff hours saved cannot be converted into dollars for a business case
D: Annual savings are $120,000, so the tool will not break even for more than four years under current assumptions
K: A
W: Savings are 12,000 hours times $50, or $600,000. Subtracting the $400,000 deployment and $100,000 running cost leaves about $100,000 net in year one, with later years netting $500,000.
X: B=One-time costs still count against the year they are incurred. | C=Loaded hourly cost is a standard way to value time saved. | D=12,000 times $50 is $600,000, not $120,000.
T: Include one-time and recurring costs when stating first-year return, and show later years separately.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.3 advanced tradeoff
S: A mid-sized insurer wants AI-assisted claims triage within six months. It has strong claims data, two ML engineers, and no plan to sell AI products. Which sourcing approach best fits?
A: Build on managed AWS AI services using its own claims data, rather than developing models from scratch
B: Develop a proprietary foundation model from scratch so the insurer fully owns every layer of the technology
C: Buy a closed third-party product that cannot use the insurer's own data, to avoid any integration work
D: Delay the initiative until the insurer can hire a research team of twenty scientists for custom development

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.3 applied comparison
S: Two competitors deploy the same public foundation model for customer service. One consistently outperforms the other in customer satisfaction. Which factor most likely explains the difference?
A: Integration with its own customer history, product data, and support workflows that the other company lacks
B: The better-performing company pays a higher per-token price for the same model, which improves its answer quality
C: The better-performing company runs the model in a different AWS Region that has newer data centers and hardware
D: The better-performing company renamed the assistant with a friendlier brand name that customers remember more easily
K: A
W: When models are the same, differentiation comes from proprietary data, workflow integration, and customer context that competitors cannot copy easily.
X: B=The same model at a different price produces the same outputs. | C=Region choice affects latency and compliance, not answer quality. | D=Branding may help adoption but does not make answers better.
T: Durable advantage comes from your data and processes, not from access to a common model.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 applied use-case
S: A marketing team reports that its AI content tool 'generated 4,000 articles this quarter.' The CFO is unimpressed. Which metric would better show business value?
A: Change in qualified leads or conversions attributed to the content compared with the prior baseline
B: The number of prompts marketers wrote each week while using the tool to generate the articles
C: The average word count of generated articles compared with the articles the team wrote by hand last year
D: The number of employees who logged in to the tool at least once during the quarter under review
K: A
W: Output volume is an activity metric. Business value is shown by outcomes the content drives, such as qualified leads or conversions against a baseline.
X: B=Prompt counts measure effort, not results. | C=Word count says nothing about impact. | D=Logins measure adoption, not value.
T: Report outcome metrics tied to business goals, not activity or usage counts.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.1 advanced tradeoff
S: A hospital network is choosing its first generative AI initiative. Option 1 summarizes internal meeting notes. Option 2 drafts patient discharge instructions sent directly to patients. Both have similar projected value. Which reasoning best guides the first choice?
A: Start with meeting summaries, which carry lower risk and let the organization build skills and governance first
B: Start with discharge instructions, because patient-facing use cases always produce the fastest return on investment
C: Start with both at once, because running two pilots in parallel always halves the time to enterprise adoption
D: Start with neither until regulators publish rules specific to generative AI in every jurisdiction the network serves
K: A
W: With similar value, a lower-risk internal use case lets the organization learn, set up governance, and prove results before exposing patients to AI-generated content.
X: B=Patient-facing content carries higher safety and regulatory risk, and faster return is not guaranteed. | C=Parallel pilots do not halve time and double the governance load. | D=Waiting for complete regulation stalls learning indefinitely.
T: When value is similar, choose the lower-risk use case first to build capability and trust.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced use-case
S: A company wants to judge whether its AI sales assistant, not a seasonal upturn, caused a 9% rise in win rates. Which evaluation design gives the most credible answer?
A: Compare a group of sales reps using the assistant with a similar group not using it over the same period
B: Compare this quarter's win rate with the same quarter five years ago, before any AI tools were in use
C: Ask the sales reps using the assistant whether they believe it helped them close more deals this quarter
D: Count how many proposals the assistant drafted, and assume each drafted proposal produced one additional win
K: A
W: A concurrent control group experiences the same season and market, so the difference between groups isolates the assistant's effect.
X: B=Five-year-old results differ in many ways besides AI. | C=Self-reports are biased and not measured outcomes. | D=Drafted proposals are activity, and assuming each became a win is unfounded.
T: Use a control group to separate AI impact from market and seasonal effects.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.3 advanced tradeoff

A: Build applications against a platform that offers several models through a common API, and keep prompts and evaluations portable
B: Sign a five-year exclusive contract with the current leading provider in exchange for a volume discount
C: Build every application on a different model provider so that no single provider ever handles more than one workload at a time
D: Avoid foundation models entirely until the market consolidates around a single long-term technology leader
K: A

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 applied comparison
S: A company is choosing KPIs for an AI document-review tool used by its legal team. Which pair combines an efficiency measure with a quality measure?
A: Review time per contract and the rate of issues missed that were later found by senior lawyers
B: Number of contracts uploaded to the tool and the number of lawyers with active user accounts
C: Monthly model inference cost and the number of different model versions tested during the quarter
D: Number of pages processed per week and the average page count of contracts reviewed by the tool
K: A
W: Review time captures efficiency, and the missed-issue rate captures quality. Tracking both prevents speed gains that quietly reduce accuracy.
X: B=These measure usage, not efficiency or quality. | C=Cost matters, but model versions tested is not a quality outcome. | D=Both are volume measures.
T: Pair speed or cost KPIs with quality KPIs so improvements are real.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.3 applied use-case

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.1 advanced use-case
S: A manufacturer's AI steering committee wants a balanced portfolio of AI initiatives. Which TWO practices support that goal? (Select TWO)
A: Mix quick, low-risk wins with a smaller number of longer-term initiatives that could create larger advantages
B: Review each initiative regularly and pause or stop those that miss agreed milestones
C: Fund every proposal equally so that no department feels its ideas were treated unfairly by the committee

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 applied tradeoff

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced troubleshooting
S: A company's AI chatbot deflected 40% of support contacts, yet support costs rose. Investigation shows customers often call after unhelpful chatbot answers. What does this reveal about the chosen KPI?
A: Deflection rate alone ignored resolution, so failed conversations created repeat contacts that raised costs
B: Deflection rate is the correct KPI, and the cost increase must be caused by unrelated seasonal factors
C: The chatbot should deflect even more contacts, since higher deflection always lowers total support costs
D: Support costs cannot be linked to chatbot performance, so no KPI can ever capture the chatbot's real business value
K: A
W: Deflection counts contacts the chatbot handled, not whether customers' problems were solved. Unresolved conversations came back as calls, so a resolution-based KPI is needed.
X: B=The investigation directly links the calls to chatbot failures. | C=Deflecting without resolving creates more repeat contacts. | D=Resolution and repeat-contact rates do link chatbot quality to cost.
T: Measure outcomes such as resolution, not proxies such as deflection, or the KPI can hide failure.
