@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.1 advanced tradeoff
S: A company can fund either an AI initiative with modest, fairly certain savings or one with large but uncertain upside. It has little AI experience. Which approach is most sensible?
A: Fund the more certain initiative first to build capability and credibility, and plan a staged test of the larger bet
B: Fund only the large uncertain initiative, since the biggest possible upside should always be pursued first
C: Fund neither until both initiatives can be guaranteed to succeed, since any failure would harm the company's reputation with investors
D: Split the budget evenly and run both at full scale immediately, regardless of the organization's experience level
K: A
W: With limited experience, a surer win builds skills, trust, and evidence, while a staged test of the bigger idea keeps its upside open with less risk.
X: B=All-in on uncertain upside is high risk for an inexperienced team. | C=No initiative can be guaranteed. | D=Running both at scale ignores capability limits.
T: Build capability with surer wins and test big bets in stages.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced use-case
S: A company deployed an AI tool to speed up contract review. After six months, review time fell 30%, but two costly clauses slipped through. How should value be assessed?
A: Weigh the time savings against the cost of the missed clauses and adjust review controls to protect quality
B: Report only the 30% time reduction, since efficiency is the only metric that executives care about
C: Stop using the tool immediately, since any error at all proves that AI cannot be used for legal work
D: Ignore the missed clauses, since human reviewers also miss clauses and some errors are therefore unavoidable in any process
K: A
W: Net value includes both gains and the cost of new errors. Measuring both and adjusting controls, such as human review of high-risk clauses, protects the gain.
X: B=Ignoring quality overstates value. | C=One failure mode can often be controlled rather than abandoning the benefit. | D=Unmanaged errors erode value and trust.
T: Net value = gains minus the cost of new errors; manage both.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 applied definition

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced tradeoff
S: An AI project will cost $300,000 per year and is expected to save $450,000 per year starting in year two, after a $200,000 setup cost in year one with no savings. Which statement about payback is correct?
A: Year one costs $500,000 with no savings; from year two it nets $150,000 a year, so payback takes over four years in total
B: The project pays back in year one, since the $450,000 savings exceed the $300,000 annual cost
C: The project never pays back, since annual costs are higher than the setup cost in every year of the project
D: The project pays back in exactly two years, since setup and running costs are recovered by the first savings
K: A
W: Year one spends $500,000 ($200,000 setup plus $300,000 running) with no savings. From year two the net gain is $150,000 a year, so recovering $500,000 takes about 3.3 years after year one, over four years in total.
X: B=No savings arrive in year one. | C=Net annual savings of $150,000 eventually recover the investment. | D=$150,000 a year does not recover $500,000 in one year.
T: Include setup costs and delayed benefits when computing payback.
