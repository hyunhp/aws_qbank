@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 applied use-case
S: A recruiting chatbot answers candidates' questions. Leadership wants to be sure it treats all candidates fairly. Which practice best supports this?
A: Test responses with varied candidate profiles and compare tone and content across groups
B: Rely on the vendor's statement that the model is unbiased, since vendors test models before release
C: Remove all candidate names from the chat window so the chatbot never sees anyone's identity at all
D: Limit the chatbot to one answer per question so that every candidate always receives an identical reply
K: A
W: Fairness should be tested in the actual use: systematically comparing responses for varied profiles reveals disparities that vendor claims cannot rule out.
X: B=Vendor testing does not cover this company's use and prompts. | C=Hiding names does not remove other signals of identity. | D=Identical short replies reduce usefulness and do not prove fairness.
T: Test fairness in your own use case with varied, representative inputs.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 advanced definition
S: A company's AI policy commits to 'robustness.' Which practice best demonstrates it?
A: Testing the system with unusual, noisy, and adversarial inputs and confirming it behaves safely
B: Publishing the system's source code on a public repository so anyone can inspect its logic
C: Reporting the system's monthly usage statistics to the board as part of the company's quarterly review
D: Choosing the largest model available, since larger models are guaranteed to be more robust in production
K: A
W: Robustness means reliable, safe behavior under unexpected or hostile inputs, which is shown by testing edge and adversarial cases.
X: B=Publishing code is transparency, not robustness. | C=Usage statistics do not show behavior under stress. | D=Size does not guarantee robustness.
T: Robustness is proven by stress-testing with unusual and adversarial inputs.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.2 applied use-case

A: Maintain an inventory of AI systems with their risk level and the regulations that apply in each market
B: Apply only the rules of the country where the company's headquarters is located to all of its AI systems worldwide
C: Wait for a single global AI law to be passed before assessing any of the company's AI systems
D: Let each engineering team interpret the regulations independently without any central oversight
K: A
W: An inventory that maps systems to risk levels and applicable regulations lets the company apply the right controls in each market.
X: B=Operating in a market means its rules apply there. | C=No single global law is imminent. | D=Independent interpretation leads to gaps and inconsistency.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.3 advanced troubleshooting

A: Indirect prompt injection; treat retrieved content as untrusted and filter it with prompt-attack detection
B: Model drift; retrain the assistant every month so that it learns to ignore instructions embedded in web pages

D: Latency risk; add caching so the assistant reads each web page only once instead of repeatedly
K: A
