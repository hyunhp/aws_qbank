# AIB-C01 batch 08: Domain 1 (fundamentals) and Domain 4 (readiness)
@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.1 applied definition
S: A sales VP hears that a lead-scoring model uses 'supervised learning.' Which description is accurate?
A: The model learned from past leads that were already labeled as converted or not converted
B: The model is monitored by a supervisor who approves every score before it reaches the sales team
C: The model groups leads into clusters without being told which past leads actually converted
D: The model is retrained by the sales team every morning using a manual spreadsheet process
K: A
W: Supervised learning trains on examples with known outcomes (labels), such as whether each past lead converted.
X: B=Human approval is oversight, not the learning type. | C=Grouping without labels is unsupervised learning. | D=Retraining schedule is unrelated to the learning type.
T: Supervised learning = learning from labeled examples.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.1 advanced use-case
S: A retailer wants to discover natural customer segments without predefined categories. Which approach fits?
A: Unsupervised clustering that groups customers by similar purchasing behavior
B: Supervised classification that assigns customers to segments the marketing team defined last year
C: Reinforcement learning that rewards the model each time a customer makes a purchase online
D: Text generation that writes a description of each customer based on their account profile
K: A
W: Discovering groups without labels is an unsupervised task; clustering finds customers with similar behavior.
X: B=Classification needs predefined labels. | C=Reinforcement learning optimizes actions, not segments. | D=Descriptions do not discover segments.
T: Use unsupervised learning to discover groups you have not defined.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.2 advanced tradeoff
S: A company wants a chatbot for employee IT questions. Answers must be fast and consistent for 50 common questions, and new questions are rare. Which approach fits best?
A: A simple intent-based bot with approved answers, adding generative AI only if needs grow
B: A custom-trained large language model built from scratch for the company's IT help desk team
C: A fully autonomous agent with access to reset any system and change any user setting
D: No automation, since IT questions are too varied for any kind of chatbot to answer well
K: A
W: For a small, stable set of questions, a rule- or intent-based bot with approved answers is cheaper, faster, and more predictable than a complex generative solution.
X: B=Training a model from scratch is excessive. | C=Broad autonomous access adds risk without need. | D=Fifty common questions are well suited to automation.
T: Choose the simplest solution that meets the need; generative AI is not always required.
V: Amazon Lex

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.3 applied definition

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.3 advanced use-case
S: A company's assistant must follow a strict response format so downstream systems can read it. Which approach improves format reliability most?
A: Give explicit format instructions with an example, and validate the output before passing it on
B: Ask the model to be creative with formatting so that outputs look more natural to downstream systems
C: Raise the temperature so the model tries several formats and picks the best one on its own
D: Remove all formatting guidance so the model uses whatever structure it prefers for each answer
K: A
W: Clear instructions and examples steer structure, and validation catches outputs that do not match before they break downstream systems.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.3 applied comparison
S: Which statement about foundation models is accurate for business planning?
A: They are trained on broad data and can be adapted to many tasks with prompting, retrieval, or fine-tuning
B: They are built for one narrow task and cannot be applied to any other use without rebuilding them
C: They learn continuously from every user conversation and update their weights in real time automatically
D: They always know a company's internal information without being given any documents or data
K: A
W: Foundation models are pre-trained broadly and adapted to many tasks. They do not automatically learn from each conversation or know private company data.
X: B=That describes traditional task-specific models. | C=Deployed models do not update weights from each chat. | D=Internal data must be provided.
T: Foundation models are broad and adaptable, but they do not know your data unless you provide it.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.1 applied use-case
S: A bank's data team says its model needs 'inference latency under 200 milliseconds.' Why does this matter to the business?
A: Card payments are approved in real time, so fraud checks must finish before the transaction completes
B: Lower latency reduces the amount of historical training data the model needs to learn fraud patterns well
C: Latency determines how accurate the model is, so faster models always catch more fraud cases
D: Regulators require every AI model in banking to respond in under 200 milliseconds by law
K: A
W: Some decisions happen in real time; if the model is too slow, it cannot be used in the transaction flow at all.
X: B=Latency does not affect training data needs. | C=Speed and accuracy are different properties. | D=There is no such universal legal requirement.
T: Latency requirements come from where the decision happens in the business process.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.2 advanced use-case

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.3 applied use-case

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.4 applied use-case
S: A company scaled an AI assistant to all regions, but results vary widely between offices. What should leadership do first?
A: Compare how offices use the tool and share the practices of high-performing offices
B: Remove the tool from the lower-performing offices immediately without investigating
C: Assume the model performs differently by region and retrain it separately for each office
D: Stop measuring results by office, since variation between offices is always random noise
K: A
W: Variation often reflects differences in process, training, or use. Understanding and spreading what works is the quickest improvement.
X: B=Removing it without analysis wastes potential value. | C=Retraining assumes a model issue without evidence. | D=Ignoring variation hides improvement opportunities.
T: When scaled results vary, learn from the best adopters before changing the technology.
