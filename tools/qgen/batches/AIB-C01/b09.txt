@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced tradeoff
S: A company runs a document-summary feature on the largest available model. Tests show a smaller model produces summaries users rate as equally good at one-fifth the cost. What should the product owner do?
A: Switch the feature to the smaller model and keep monitoring quality ratings after the change
B: Keep the largest model, since the biggest model always gives the business the most value per request
C: Use both models on every request and show users two summaries so they can pick the one they prefer
D: Remove the summary feature, since any feature running on a foundation model is too expensive to justify
K: A
W: When quality is equal for the task, the cheaper model delivers the same value at lower cost. Monitoring confirms quality holds in production.
X: B=Larger models cost more and are not always better for a given task. | C=Running both doubles cost and burdens users. | D=The smaller model makes the feature affordable.
T: Match model size to the task; bigger is not automatically better value.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.3 advanced use-case

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.2 advanced use-case
S: A company's AI initiative has spent $2 million and results are poor. Managers argue it must continue 'because we have already invested so much.' How should leadership decide?
A: Decide based on expected future costs and benefits, not on money already spent
B: Continue funding at any cost, since stopping would waste the $2 million already invested
C: Double the budget immediately, since more spending always improves poor AI results
D: Blame the vendor publicly and continue the project unchanged with the same plan
K: A
W: Money already spent cannot be recovered; the decision should weigh only future costs against future benefits. Avoiding the sunk cost fallacy prevents further losses.
X: B=This is the sunk cost fallacy. | C=Spending more does not fix a flawed approach. | D=Blame does not change the economics.
T: Ignore sunk costs; decide on future costs and benefits.

@ AIB-C01 D2 T2.1 advanced use-case
S: A company must decide whether a use case even needs AI. Which TWO signals suggest AI is a good fit? (Select TWO)
A: The task involves patterns in large volumes of data that are hard to capture with explicit rules
B: Some errors are tolerable and can be caught by review or correction processes
C: Every output must be exactly reproducible and follow a published formula without any exception at all
D: The company has no data related to the task and no way of collecting any in the future
E: The task happens once a year and takes a single employee about ten minutes to complete
K: A,B
W: AI fits when patterns are too complex for rules and when occasional errors can be managed. Exact, rule-defined, data-less, or trivial tasks rarely justify AI.
X: C=Exact formula-driven tasks fit deterministic software. | D=Without data, most AI approaches cannot work. | E=The effort far exceeds the benefit.
T: Use AI where patterns are complex and errors are manageable.
