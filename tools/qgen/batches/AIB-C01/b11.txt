# AIB-C01 batch 11: Domain 3 (governance and responsible AI)
@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 applied use-case
S: A company plans an AI tool that suggests salaries for new hires based on past offers. What is the main responsible AI concern?
A: Past offers may reflect historical pay gaps, which the model could learn and repeat
B: Salary suggestions will take too long to generate for hiring managers during interviews
C: The tool may use too much storage because salary records are usually very large files
D: Hiring managers may prefer a different font on the page that shows salary suggestions
K: A
W: Models trained on historical decisions can reproduce historical inequities. Pay data should be checked for gaps and outcomes monitored.
X: B=Speed is not the main concern. | C=Salary records are small. | D=Presentation is cosmetic.
T: Historical decisions can encode bias that models will learn.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 advanced tradeoff
S: A company's content moderation model can be tuned to catch more harmful posts at the cost of wrongly removing more legitimate posts. How should leadership approach the setting?
A: Choose the balance deliberately based on the harms of each error type, and provide an appeal path
B: Always maximize removal of harmful content, since wrongly removed posts have no real consequences
C: Always minimize wrongful removals, since harmful content is the users' own responsibility to avoid
D: Let the model vendor pick the threshold, since vendors know best what each company's users need
K: A
W: Both error types cause harm. Leadership should weigh them for the platform's context, set the threshold accordingly, and let people contest mistakes.
X: B=Wrongful removals harm users and trust. | C=Ignoring harmful content harms users and the platform. | D=The company is accountable for its own policy choices.
T: Set AI thresholds deliberately by weighing the harms of each error type.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.2 applied definition
S: What is the purpose of an 'AI system inventory' in an AI governance program?
A: To record which AI systems exist, their owners, purposes, and risk levels so they can be overseen
B: To list the laptops, servers, and GPUs that the data science team uses for its model development work
C: To track the office supplies purchased for the AI center of excellence each quarter
D: To catalog the AI conferences employees attended during the year for training budgets
K: A
W: An inventory gives governance visibility: what AI is in use, who owns it, what it does, and how risky it is.
X: B=Hardware lists are IT asset management. | C=Supplies are unrelated. | D=Conference tracking is training administration.
T: You cannot govern AI you have not inventoried.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.2 advanced use-case
S: A company buys an AI product from a vendor for use in hiring. Which governance responsibility remains with the company?
A: Assessing and monitoring how the product performs and affects candidates in the company's own hiring
B: None, since the vendor is fully responsible for any outcomes produced by its AI product
C: Only paying the vendor's invoices on time, since use of the product is the vendor's concern
D: Only training recruiters on the product's interface, since fairness is purely a technical matter for the vendor
K: A
W: Organizations deploying AI remain accountable for its use and effects in their context, even when the product comes from a vendor.
X: B=Accountability for use stays with the deployer. | C=Payment is not governance. | D=Fairness in use is the company's responsibility too.
T: Buying AI does not outsource accountability for how it is used.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.3 applied use-case

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 applied use-case

B: Make responses as long as possible so users feel the app is thorough and caring
C: Hide the fact that responses are AI-generated so users feel more comfortable

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.2 advanced use-case

B: Retain logs that show how systems behaved and which controls were active

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.3 applied comparison

A: Taking an unintended real-world action, such as issuing a refund or deleting a record
B: Producing an answer that uses more words than necessary for the user's question
C: Responding in a formal tone when the user expected a more casual conversational tone

K: A

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.3 advanced tradeoff
S: A company can deploy an AI feature now with basic safeguards or wait three months to add stronger testing. The feature affects customer credit limits. Which choice is most responsible?
A: Complete stronger testing first, or launch in a limited, closely monitored way with human review
B: Launch to all customers immediately, since competitors may release similar features soon
C: Launch without safeguards, since customers can always call support if a credit limit is wrong
D: Cancel the feature permanently, since any use of AI in credit decisions is unacceptable to all regulators
K: A
W: Credit decisions significantly affect customers. Stronger testing, or a limited launch with human review, reduces the risk of harm while still progressing.
X: B=Competitive pressure does not justify unmanaged risk to customers. | C=Relying on complaints shifts harm onto customers. | D=Well-governed AI can be used in credit.
T: Match launch caution to the impact on people.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 applied definition
S: A project sponsor asks what the company's commitment to 'privacy by design' means for a new AI project. Which description is accurate?
A: Building data protection into the system from the start, such as collecting only needed data
B: Adding a privacy policy link to the company's website after the AI system has already launched to customers
C: Keeping all AI projects secret so that nobody outside the team knows they exist
D: Encrypting the model's source code so competitors cannot copy the algorithm
K: A
W: Privacy by design means considering data protection throughout design and development, including minimization and access controls.
X: B=Adding a link after launch is not design. | C=Secrecy is not privacy protection. | D=Protecting code is IP protection, not privacy.
T: Build privacy into AI systems from the start.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.3 applied use-case
S: Which TWO signals should trigger a review of a deployed AI system? (Select TWO)
A: A noticeable change in the system's accuracy or error patterns
B: A significant change in the population or data the system serves
C: A new team member joining the department that uses the system
D: The system's dashboard being moved to a different office monitor
E: The quarterly all-hands meeting being rescheduled to another date
K: A,B
W: Changes in performance or in the data and population served are key signals that a system may no longer be safe or effective.
X: C=Staffing changes do not affect the system's behavior. | D=Display changes are irrelevant. | E=Meeting schedules are irrelevant.
T: Review AI when performance or the population it serves changes.
