# AIB-C01 batch 12: Domain 4 (readiness and transformation)
@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.1 applied use-case
S: A company's leaders want to know whether the organization can support an AI initiative. Which assessment area is most often overlooked?
A: Whether the processes and people that must change to use the AI output are ready to change
B: Whether the company's logo will look good on the AI project's internal status dashboard and reports
C: Whether the office Wi-Fi is fast enough for employees to read project update emails
D: Whether the project team has enough desks in the same room for daily stand-up meetings
K: A
W: AI only creates value when workflows and people adopt its outputs; organizational readiness is frequently underestimated compared with technology readiness.
X: B=Branding is irrelevant. | C=Email Wi-Fi is not a readiness factor. | D=Seating is a logistics detail.
T: Assess process and people readiness, not just technology.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.1 advanced use-case
S: An AI maturity assessment shows a company has strong data platforms but little experience deploying models to production. Which next step fits best?
A: Build MLOps practices through a small production deployment with monitoring and support
B: Buy additional data storage, since the assessment found data platforms to be the main gap
C: Start ten complex AI projects at once so teams gain experience as quickly as possible
D: Stop all AI work until the company hires a large team that already has production experience
K: A
W: The gap is production capability. A focused deployment that builds deployment, monitoring, and support practices addresses it directly.
X: B=Data platforms are already strong. | C=Many complex projects at once overwhelm a team without production experience. | D=Waiting delays learning.
T: Close the specific maturity gap the assessment finds.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.2 applied use-case
S: A company wants to use AI across departments, but each department stores customer data under different definitions of 'active customer.' Which foundational step helps most?
A: Agree on shared business definitions and a data catalog that documents them
B: Let each department keep its own definition and have the AI decide which one to use
C: Build a separate model for each department's definition and compare results monthly
D: Remove the 'active customer' field from all datasets so there is nothing to disagree on
K: A
W: Shared definitions, documented in a catalog, make data consistent across departments so AI outputs are comparable and trustworthy.
X: B=AI cannot resolve business definitions on its own. | C=Separate models multiply inconsistency. | D=Removing a key field loses information.
T: Align business definitions before scaling AI across departments.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.2 advanced tradeoff
S: A company must choose between building a modern data platform first or starting an AI use case on existing messy data. Which approach usually works best?
A: Start a focused use case while improving the data it needs, so platform work is guided by real needs
B: Build a complete enterprise data platform over several years before attempting any AI use case at all
C: Start many AI use cases on the messy data and accept whatever accuracy the models produce
D: Buy a new AI tool that promises to work perfectly without any data preparation at all
K: A
W: Pairing a valuable use case with targeted data improvements delivers results sooner and prioritizes platform work that matters.
X: B=Long platform-first efforts delay value and may build the wrong things. | C=Ignoring data quality produces poor results. | D=No tool removes the need for data preparation.
T: Let valuable use cases drive incremental data foundation work.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.3 applied use-case
S: Middle managers are resisting an AI tool that changes how their teams work. What is the most effective leadership response?
A: Involve managers in designing the new workflows and define how their teams' success will be measured
B: Bypass managers and communicate directly with their teams to speed up the rollout
C: Replace the managers who resist with new hires who are already enthusiastic about using AI tools at work
D: Delay the rollout until every manager voluntarily asks for the tool on their own
K: A
W: Managers shape how teams work. Involving them in workflow design and success measures turns them into advocates.
X: B=Bypassing managers increases resistance. | C=Replacement is disruptive and costly. | D=Waiting for voluntary requests stalls change.
T: Bring middle managers into the design of AI-enabled work.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.3 advanced use-case
S: A company is creating an AI center of excellence. Which TWO responsibilities fit this group best? (Select TWO)
A: Set shared standards, reusable components, and best practices for AI projects
B: Support business teams with expertise while those teams own their use cases
C: Build and run every AI system in the company without involving business teams
D: Approve all hiring decisions for the company, since hiring affects AI readiness
E: Replace the finance team's budgeting process with an AI-driven process immediately
K: A,B
W: A center of excellence accelerates AI by providing standards, reusable assets, and expert support, while business teams stay accountable for outcomes.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.4 applied use-case
S: A pilot showed strong results with a highly motivated team. What is the main risk when scaling to the whole company?
A: Other teams may be less engaged or have different processes, so results may not repeat
B: The model will become less accurate simply because many more employees are using it every day
C: The AI vendor will refuse to support more than one team using the same product
D: Scaling always doubles the model's accuracy, which may surprise business users
K: A
W: Pilot teams are often enthusiastic and well supported. Broader rollout meets different contexts, so adoption and results must be managed and measured.
X: B=More users do not reduce model accuracy by themselves. | C=Vendors typically support scaling. | D=Scaling does not change accuracy.
T: Pilot results may not generalize; plan for different teams and contexts.

@ AIB-C01 D4 T4.4 advanced use-case
S: A company scaled an AI assistant, and costs grew faster than usage because each team configured it differently. Which step best controls this?
A: Standardize configurations and shared components, and monitor cost per use across teams
B: Allow each team to keep customizing freely, since flexibility always reduces overall costs
C: Remove the assistant from teams with the highest costs without investigating the causes
D: Stop tracking costs by team, since total spending is the only number that matters
K: A
W: Standard configurations and shared components reduce duplication, and unit-cost monitoring highlights where spending is inefficient.
X: B=Unmanaged customization caused the problem. | C=Removing without analysis may cut valuable use. | D=Per-team visibility is needed to fix inefficiency.
T: Standardize and monitor unit costs when scaling AI.

A: Technical readiness covers data and infrastructure; organizational readiness covers skills, processes, and leadership

C: They are the same concept, measured twice by different departments for budgeting purposes
