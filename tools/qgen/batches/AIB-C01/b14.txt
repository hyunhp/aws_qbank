@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.1 applied use-case
S: A model that detects damaged parcels from photos performs well in daylight but poorly on night-shift photos. What is the most likely cause?
A: Training images were mostly taken in daylight, so night conditions were underrepresented
B: The model automatically switches off its accuracy features during the night shift hours
C: Night-shift employees upload photos in a file format that models are unable to read at all
D: Image models only work in daylight because they rely on sunlight to detect the parcel edges
K: A
W: Models perform best on conditions similar to their training data. Underrepresented conditions, such as low light, lead to poor performance.
X: B=Models do not have time-based accuracy switches. | C=File format is not the stated problem and is easily handled. | D=Models can work at night if trained on such images.
T: Cover all real operating conditions in training data.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.1 advanced tradeoff
S: A fraud team can lower the model's alert threshold to catch more fraud, but analysts can only review 500 alerts a day. How should the threshold be chosen?
A: Balance fraud caught against analyst capacity and the cost of each type of error
B: Always use the lowest threshold, since catching every possible fraud case is the only goal
C: Always use the highest threshold, since fewer alerts always means a better fraud program
D: Choose the threshold at random each week so that fraudsters cannot predict the setting
K: A
W: Thresholds trade recall against workload and false alarms. The right setting reflects business costs and operational capacity.
X: B=Too many alerts overwhelm analysts and real fraud gets missed in the queue. | C=Too few alerts let fraud through. | D=Random thresholds make performance unpredictable.
T: Set model thresholds by weighing error costs and operational capacity.

@ AIB-C01 D1 T1.1 applied comparison

A: Classification predicts a category, such as approve or decline; regression predicts a number, such as a price
B: Classification predicts a number, such as next month's revenue; regression predicts a category label for each case
C: Classification works only with images, while regression works only with text and tabular data
D: Classification requires no training data, while regression requires large labeled training datasets
K: A
W: Classification assigns categories; regression estimates continuous values.

C: Mobile app data cannot be used in models, so the finding must be discarded immediately
