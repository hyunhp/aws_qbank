G: ## What a model or service card is
A card is a structured disclosure that describes what an AI model or service is meant to do, how it was evaluated, where it performs poorly and what design choices were made for fairness, privacy and safety. AWS publishes AI Service Cards for several of its managed AI services for this purpose, and many model providers publish model cards.
## What a card is not
A card is an input to the buyer's own risk assessment. It does not transfer accountability: the company that puts the feature in front of customers still owns the outcome, so it must test the model on its own data and use case.
W: Documented intended uses and limitations let the team compare the provider's stated scope against the planned summarization use, find gaps such as unsupported languages or domains, and decide what extra testing, guardrails or human review are needed. X: B=Card as certification: a card describes the provider's testing, not the buyer's context. Skipping the company's own evaluation would leave use-case-specific risks, such as domain vocabulary or customer demographics, untested.
C=Price only: transparency documents are exactly how a buyer learns about limitations and evaluation scope. Discarding them removes the main evidence available for a responsible-AI comparison.
D=Prefer the brochure: a headline number without test conditions cannot be compared reliably, and a candid limitations section is a sign of maturity, not weakness.
T: Model and service cards are evidence for the buyer's own fit and risk assessment, not a guarantee that replaces testing.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.1 advanced use-case
S: A claims team lets an AI system draft approval recommendations that adjusters then confirm. A review shows adjusters accept the AI recommendation in almost every case within seconds, so human oversight has become a formality. Which change would best restore meaningful oversight?
A: Route low-confidence and high-impact cases to reviewers who see the evidence and the model's uncertainty, and track override rates and review time.

C: Add a second confirmation click for every recommendation, so each adjuster must acknowledge the AI output twice before the claim moves to the next stage.

G: ## Automation bias
When people see a confident AI recommendation, they tend to accept it without checking. This is called automation bias. Having a human in the workflow does not create oversight by itself; the reviewer needs the information, time and authority to disagree.
## Designing oversight that works
- Send the cases where the model is unsure or where a wrong decision does serious harm to a person who has enough context to judge.
- Show evidence and uncertainty, not just a verdict.
- Measure oversight itself: how often reviewers override, how long they spend, and whether overrides correlate with later errors.
W: Confidence-based routing, evidence display and oversight metrics turn a rubber stamp into a real control. Reviewers spend attention where the model is least reliable, and leaders can see whether the review still adds value.
X: B=Remove the human step: fast acceptance proves reviewers are not adding scrutiny, not that the model is right. Removing the step eliminates the safeguard for exactly the cases where the model fails.
C=Second click: an extra acknowledgement adds friction without adding information. Reviewers would click twice just as quickly, and the underlying automation bias stays untouched.
D=Scripted junior reviewers: a fixed script discourages judgement and cannot handle unusual cases. Oversight depends on reviewers with authority and context, not on uniform handling.
T: Meaningful human oversight needs routing, evidence, authority and measurement, otherwise it becomes automation bias with a signature.

No generative AI system can be proven to never fail. Responsible release means understanding the residual risk, reducing it with guardrails and testing, and keeping the ability to detect problems and roll back.

D=Developer prompts only: builders test the paths they expect and share the same blind spots. Adversarial testing needs different perspectives and hostile intent to be informative.

C=Single delete command: weights are a compressed statistical result of many examples. There is no entry for one customer to delete, so the file being single does not help.
D=Retrieval copies into provider training: retrieved documents are supplied as context for a single request and are not added to a provider's training set under Amazon Bedrock's data commitments.

@ AIB-C01 D3 T3.2 advanced tradeoff
S: A company has an AI governance board of legal, security, data science and business leaders. Model owners complain that nobody knows who makes the final call on launching a customer-facing AI feature, and launches stall in committee. Which RACI-based change best fixes this?
A: Name one accountable executive per use case, have the board run defined risk reviews within a set time, and consult or inform other functions without giving each a veto.

G: ## RACI in AI governance
RACI assigns each activity four roles: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (single owner of the outcome, who has the final say), Consulted (gives input before a decision) and Informed (told after). A well-formed RACI has exactly one Accountable party per decision.

When accountability is shared or unclear, every reviewer can block and nobody can approve. Governance should be proportionate: high-risk use cases get a full review, and lower-risk ones follow lighter paths with clear time limits.
W: One accountable executive per use case removes ambiguity, the board runs defined risk reviews on time, and other functions are consulted or informed, so risk is reviewed without committee gridlock.
X: B=Everyone accountable: accountability split across all members means no one owns the decision, and unlimited vetoes produce the very stalls the teams describe.
C=Self-approval: model owners have an incentive to launch and lack independent perspective. Governance needs separation between the builder and the risk reviewer.
D=Single senior decider with no consultation: the decision maker would lack legal, security and technical input, and the model owners would be informed only after the fact, which weakens quality.
T: A workable RACI names a single accountable owner per decision, time-boxed reviews and consulted, not vetoing, stakeholders.
