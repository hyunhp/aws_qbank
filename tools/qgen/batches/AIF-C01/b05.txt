@ AIF-C01 D4 T4.1 advanced use-case
S: A loan model was trained on data where one group is heavily underrepresented. Which responsible AI concern arises most directly?
A: The model may perform worse or unfairly for that group
B: The model will train faster because it has fewer records
C: The model cannot be deployed to any AWS Region at all
D: The model will automatically correct the imbalance itself
K: A
W: Underrepresented groups often receive less accurate or biased predictions; rebalancing data and measuring outcomes by group help.
X: B=Training speed is not the concern. | C=Deployment is not blocked by imbalance. | D=Models do not self-correct imbalance.
T: Underrepresentation can cause unfair model behavior.

@ AIF-C01 D4 T4.2 advanced tradeoff
S: A healthcare company must choose between an interpretable model with slightly lower accuracy and a complex model with slightly higher accuracy. Clinicians must understand each recommendation. Which choice fits?
A: The interpretable model, since understanding each recommendation is required
B: The complex model, since the highest accuracy always outweighs interpretability
C: Neither, since no model can ever be used for any healthcare recommendation
D: The complex model, with explanations invented later by the support team
K: A
W: When understanding recommendations is required and the accuracy gap is small, interpretability should take priority.
X: B=Accuracy is not always decisive. | C=Models can be used responsibly with oversight. | D=Invented explanations are not real explanations.
T: Prefer interpretable models when explanations are required and accuracy costs are small.

@ AIF-C01 D4 T4.2 applied comparison
S: Which statement correctly contrasts transparent (interpretable) models with black-box models?
A: Interpretable models show how inputs lead to outputs; black-box models are harder to explain
B: Interpretable models are always more accurate than black-box models on every task
C: Black-box models cannot be deployed on AWS, while interpretable models can be
D: Interpretable models require no data, while black-box models require training data
K: A
W: Interpretable models, such as linear models or small trees, show their reasoning; complex models often need separate explanation methods.
X: B=Interpretable models are not always more accurate. | C=Both can be deployed on AWS. | D=Both require data.
T: Interpretability is a trade-off with complexity, not a guarantee of accuracy.
