# SAA-C03 batch 03: Domain 3 (high-performing architectures)
@ SAA-C03 D3 T3.1 applied use-case
S: An application needs block storage with consistent high IOPS for a transactional database on EC2. Which EBS volume type fits best?
A: Provisioned IOPS SSD (io2)
B: Throughput Optimized HDD (st1)
C: Cold HDD storage volumes (sc1)
D: Magnetic previous-generation disks
K: A
W: io2 volumes deliver consistent, high IOPS for demanding transactional databases.
X: B=st1 targets sequential throughput. | C=sc1 targets infrequent access. | D=Magnetic volumes are low-performance legacy storage.
T: High, consistent IOPS → Provisioned IOPS SSD.
V: Amazon EBS

@ SAA-C03 D3 T3.1 advanced use-case
S: A big data job reads large files sequentially from block storage at high throughput, and cost matters. Which EBS type fits?
A: Throughput Optimized HDD (st1)
B: Provisioned IOPS SSD (io2)
C: General Purpose SSD (gp3)
D: Cold HDD storage (sc1)
K: A
W: st1 is designed for frequently accessed, throughput-intensive sequential workloads at low cost.
X: B=io2 is costly and optimized for IOPS. | C=gp3 is general purpose and costs more per GB for this use. | D=sc1 is for infrequently accessed data.
T: Sequential throughput at low cost → st1.
V: Amazon EBS

@ SAA-C03 D3 T3.1 advanced use-case
S: Large files uploaded to S3 often fail midway over unreliable networks. Which approach improves reliability and speed?
A: Multipart upload with parallel parts and retries
B: Single PUT requests with longer client timeouts
C: Compressing files into one larger archive object
D: Uploading files one byte at a time in sequence
K: A
W: Multipart upload sends parts in parallel and retries only failed parts.
X: B=Single PUTs restart entirely on failure. | C=Larger objects increase failure impact. | D=Byte-by-byte uploads are impractical.
T: Large uploads → multipart upload.
V: Amazon S3
